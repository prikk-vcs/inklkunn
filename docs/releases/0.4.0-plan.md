## 1. In one paragraph

**0.4.0 makes each wiki's rules explicit, versioned and changeable by proposal. It also gives maintainers moderation
that hides content without erasing it.**
- **Unlike 0.3.0, it is not built yet.** The cycle is RFC 006 (the design and your rulings), its build, then readiness.
- **The scope is AC-M4, cut to what a wiki needs in order to govern itself.** Everything else in §8 of the
  requirements waits for a later minor. Section 2 says what goes where, and why it can wait.
- **One item of AC-M4 is proposed to move** (P-1): the tool that permanently removes content from a repository's
  history. It moves to the operations RFC, and keeps the requirement's own deadline: before any public deployment.
- **The scope is frozen when you authorize this plan.** A defect found during the cycle is fixed in 0.4.0. Anything
  else goes later.

## 2. Scope

**What exists today** (0.3.0): six roles per wiki (observer, questioner, contributor, reviewer, maintainer, admin).
They are fixed in code and granted by an admin, and `authorize()` checks every path against them. RFC 004's bootstrap
rule needs no approvals, and lets a maintainer accept their own proposal in a wiki with fewer than three maintainers.
There are rate limits and quotas, and the participation counts are recorded but not shown. A page can be removed by an
accepted proposal and brought back by a rollback. A wiki has a default license, and a page may name its own. An action called *moderate* exists,
but nothing uses it.

**In 0.4.0** (RFC 006):

| | What | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Policy is data.** A wiki's rules live in a validated file under `governance/` in its own repository, so they are versioned and signed like a page. There are four layers. The operator's platform constitution, set in configuration, holds the limits no wiki can exceed. The wiki's constitution can be changed only by its admins. The wiki's policy is changed by the rule it states itself. Object-level settings (b, d) come last. `authorize()` reads the policy instead of the fixed table. **The default policy is exactly today's behaviour,** so a wiki upgraded from 0.3.0 changes nothing until someone proposes a change | GV-1 |
| b | **The ladder as policy.** The six roles stay the rungs. The policy says which rung each action needs, within the constitution's limits. It also says how many approvals an acceptance needs, whether self-acceptance is allowed (RFC 004 D-3 becomes data), who creates views, and the review interval for sources (RFC 005 D-10 moves from the operator to the wiki). Promotion criteria are written in the policy, and a promotion is a recorded decision that names them. Nothing promotes automatically, and there is no score (C-12) | GV-2 |
| c | **A policy change is a proposal.** Governance files are proposed, reviewed and accepted like any change, and validated when they are accepted. A change that would leave the wiki ungovernable is refused: no rung could change the constitution, an action no rung can take, or an approval count no one can meet. **Every decision names the role and the policy version that allowed it** | GV-1, GV-3 |
| d | **Protections.** A page or a namespace can require review, or restrict acceptance to a rung. Each protection has a reason and an expiry (time-limited by default), and is shown on the page with its reason | GV-4 |
| e | **Moderation hides; it does not erase.** A comment, a thread entry, a proposal's text or summary or author, or a revision's content in the history views can be hidden behind a *tombstone*. The tombstone keeps the item's place, states the reason category and the acting role, is logged and can be reversed. inklkunn's pages and API stop serving the hidden content. What prikk holds does not change | GV-5 |
| f | **Appeals.** Every decline, moderation action and role decision has a way to contest it. An appeal is recorded, listed for the admins, and decided with a recorded outcome | GV-8 |
| g | **Soft deletion,** with **retiring a claim or a source** (deferred from 0.3.0). A removed page is shown as removed: when, by which accepted proposal, and why, with a way to propose bringing it back. Links to it become dangling links, never dead ends. A claim or a source is retired in the same way, and its pages say so | GV-6 (soft), HIST-7 |
| h | **Small items recorded for 0.4.0,** run now as one maintenance package, before RFC 006 is accepted (P-3): *Why is this here?* lists the sources attached by the change (RFC 005 D-8 in full); the signer reads a `refusal-v1` on a non-zero exit as prikk's refusal; a doctor line with an unknown severity is refused; the joined image tag is attested, or RELEASING.md says which tag a reader verifies | — |
| i | **Upstream re-baselines** as prikk and planeter release (RFC 010). As with 0.58.0, a release before the cut is tested, and only a defect holds the cut | — |

**Not in 0.4.0:**

| Item | Where it goes | Why it can wait |
|---|---|---|
| **The permanent removal procedure:** a repository re-created without some content, re-signed, with its ids and permalinks changed (GV-6, register item 14) | **The operations RFC (T11), with backup and a rehearsed restore, and IS-15's `recover`.** The requirement's own deadline stays: *before any public deployment* (P-1) | It is a rewrite of the whole history under the wiki's key, and it belongs with the other procedures that rebuild a repository. Hiding (e) covers what moderation needs until there are readers. There are no production users or public deployments (owner, 2026-11-18) |
| Progressive friction for new accounts; spam and sock-puppet signals for maintainers (GV-7 beyond today's rate limits and quotas) | a later minor, after GV-9 | The signals are read from the same measurements as GV-9. Today's limits stay |
| The inclusiveness view for maintainers (GV-9) | a later minor | The counts have been recorded since 0.2.0, so nothing is lost by waiting |
| A license per attachment, and relicensing as an event (GV-10 beyond what ships today: the wiki's default and a page's own license) | RFC 007 (M5, interchange) | Every page already declares its license. The rest is about what leaves the wiki |
| Governance forks; free-standing page discussion (both named "RFC 006's" by RFC 005) | a later minor | A view and a claim's thread already cover disagreement about content. Page discussion is a discussion feature, not a rule |
| Attaching an existing source to another claim | a later minor | A recorded gap from 0.3.0: it costs a duplicate file, never a wrong one |
| IS-18: a restricted wiki's raw source when the content origin has its own host | RFC 008 (shared identity with planeter), or the operations RFC, whichever comes first | It is about sign-in across origins, not governance. It fails closed, and the page itself reads |
| Reading threads and view records back on import | RFC 007 (M5) | Export carries them |
| A second factor on local accounts; a run against a real OpenID Connect provider; an external security review | before 1.0 | As recorded at 0.3.0 |

**Not gates of 0.4.0** (as for 0.3.0):
- the Japanese catalog (DR-7), which ships as a labelled preview unless your sign-off comes first;
- the test by a person using assistive technology, planned before 1.0 or the first public deployment.

## 3. Your decisions in this plan

- **P-1 — The removal tool moves out of 0.4.0,** to the operations RFC, keeping *before any public deployment*.
  AC-M4's *the removal procedure documented and rehearsed* becomes *planned there*. **Recommended.**
- **P-2 — GV-7 beyond today, GV-9, the rest of GV-10, governance forks and page discussion are not in
  0.4.0** (section 2's table). **Recommended.**
- **P-3 — The small items (2h) run now,** as a maintenance package. The dev team is otherwise idle until RFC 006 is
  accepted. **Recommended.**

RFC 006 brings its own design questions for your rulings. These are expected:
- Do role grants (who holds which rung) stay in the server's store, or go into the repository? The architect will
  recommend the store: grants name accounts, and the history can never forget a name.
- Does a revision hidden by moderation still leave in the export bundle?
- Is a protection a direct, logged maintainer action, or a proposal?
- What can the platform constitution forbid a wiki?

## 4. Exit criteria (all must hold on the candidate commit)

1. **Gates green on the candidate:**
   - the architect's gates: fmt, clippy, the `+1.89.0` check, rustdoc, deny, audit, and the tests with prikk 0.55.0,
     shimmed off, on 0.57.0, and on the newest prikk asset;
   - CI;
   - the release rehearsal: gates, both binaries, both images run confined, the manifest.
2. **Threat model v0.4** covers what 0.4.0 adds, and its §7 tests are re-verified on the candidate. It covers:
   - authority taken by a policy change;
   - a policy that locks the wiki;
   - moderation used to silence;
   - hidden content leaking through the history, a diff, the raw source, a permalink, the API or the export;
   - an appeal used for flooding.
3. **The readiness report** is reviewed by the architect, with every RFC 006 acceptance criterion shown by a named
   test.
4. **The end-to-end proof on a real prikk**, in the shape of 0.3.0's R-2:
   - an accepted policy change changes who may accept;
   - a protection is shown and expires;
   - a hidden comment and a hidden revision are not served, and the hiding is reversed;
   - an appeal is decided;
   - a page is removed and restored;
   - a claim is retired.
5. **No open High issue.**
6. **An upgrade from 0.3.0 is shown.** A data directory made by the 0.3.0 release binary is opened by the candidate.
   Its wikis behave exactly as before under the default policy, and a policy change then takes effect.
7. **The release notes' lead** says in a reader's words what 0.4.0 adds and what it does not do.
8. **You authorize the cut.**
