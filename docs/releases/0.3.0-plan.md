## 1. In one paragraph

**0.3.0 is already built; what remains is proving it and saying it.**
- Every task of RFC 005 part 2 (K-T1 – K-T11, D-1 – D-12) is implemented, reviewed and approved, and both correction series have ended.
- `main` is green on Ubuntu. The release rehearsal of 2026-11-17 ran the image confined on both architectures.
- **No feature is added to 0.3.0.** The work below is readiness: a threat model that covers what M3 added, an end-to-end proof on a real prikk, the upgrade from 0.2.0, notes a reader can use, and a rehearsal of the candidate.
- **The scope is frozen from today.** A defect found during readiness is fixed in 0.3.0. Anything else goes to 0.4.0.

## 2. Scope

**In 0.3.0** (M3, as built):
- **Claims and sources** as small files beside the pages, made and changed only by proposals, with a curation form.
- **What a reader sees:** *Why is this here?* for a sentence (traced through the history, labelled when matched by text), a claim's sources and their bearing, and other views with a comparison. No word or number that says *true*.
- **Freshness** (*Sources to review*) and **a claim's thread**.
- **Interchange:** the knowledge files in the exported bundle, threads included, under a declared extension; the API for all of it, with OpenAPI.
- **The November 2026 maintenance:**
  - the sandbox takes the host's own layout (IS-19), so prikk runs confined on Debian and Ubuntu and in the image;
  - the release runs the image confined before publishing;
  - SECURITY.md says what a host and a container need;
  - the test-only prikk 0.57.0 is checked in CI.

**Not in 0.3.0** (each already recorded, with where it goes):

| Item | Where it goes | Why it can wait |
|---|---|---|
| Removing or retiring a claim or a source; replacing a file that cannot be read | RFC 006 (M4, 0.4.0) | Ruled 2026-11-14; every surface says so plainly |
| Attaching an existing source to another claim, instead of a new one | after 0.3.0 (a recorded gap) | A new source works; the gap costs a duplicate file, not a wrong one |
| Reading threads and view records back on import | RFC 007 (M5) | Export carries them; nothing is lost |
| IS-18: a restricted wiki's raw source when the content origin has its own host | RFC 006 (M4), with sign-in across origins | It fails closed (*not found*), and the page itself reads |
| Native Unicode paths in prikk (ledger PK-21) | when prikk ships them | Percent-encoded paths work and are reversible |
| A patch release of 0.1.0 or 0.2.0 for IS-19 | none (owner, 2026-11-18: no production users) | Fixed forward in 0.3.0 |

**Not gates of 0.3.0** (authorized 2026-11-18):
- **The Japanese catalog (DR-7).** It ships as a labelled preview again unless your sign-off comes before the cut. Your review is not a gate.
- **A person using assistive technology testing the site.** The automated WCAG 2.2 AA structural tests remain gates. No such person is arranged, and there are no production users, so I propose moving this from *before 0.3.0* to **before 1.0 or the first public deployment, whichever comes first**. The about page keeps saying it has not been done.
- **prikk 0.58.0.** If it is released before the cut, it is re-baselined (RFC 010). The cut does not wait for it.

## 3. Exit criteria (all must hold on the candidate commit)

1. **Gates green on the candidate:**
   - the architect's gates: fmt, clippy, the `+1.89.0` check, rustdoc, deny, audit, and the tests with prikk 0.55.0, shimmed off, on 0.57.0, and on the release asset;
   - CI;
   - **the release rehearsal on the candidate** (gates, both binaries, both images run confined, manifest).
2. **Threat model v0.3** covers what 0.3.0 adds, and **its §7 tests are re-verified** on the candidate (as at 0.1.0 and 0.2.0).
3. **The readiness report** (section 4, R-2 – R-5) is reviewed by the architect, with every RFC 005 part 2 acceptance criterion shown by a named test.
4. **No open High issue** in the register.
5. **An upgrade from 0.2.0 is shown:** a data directory made by the 0.2.0 release binary is opened by the candidate, the store migrates (schema 15 → 18), and its wikis, accounts, keys and open proposals work.
6. **The release notes' lead** says, in a reader's words, what 0.3.0 adds and what it does not do. The full section stays one link away.
7. **The owner authorizes the cut.**

## 8. prikk 0.58.0, and the cut held (2026-11-18)

prikk 0.58.0 was released after the cut proposal (section 7). The architect ran the whole workspace on the candidate
against it: **3,082 passed, 23 failed**, all in recovery.

**One of the failures is a defect of 0.3.0 against the current prikk.** 0.58.0's `unlock --yes` refuses a lock whose
holder's liveness it cannot establish: an empty lock, or one written by an older prikk. So inklkunn's recovery stops
at *clear-locks*, and the wiki stays flagged until an operator clears the lock by hand. It fails closed. But 0.58.0 is
what a new operator installs, and 0.3.0 says *prikk 0.54.0 or later*. **The proposal of section 7 is therefore
withdrawn.**

- **The fix is in 0.3.0**, by section 1's rule (a defect found during readiness is fixed in 0.3.0). It is one small
  package, M50 – M55 in the maintenance handoff:
  - recovery clears the lock with `--force` on its own lease's word, which prikk confirmed is right;
  - the cleanup-pending state and the debris notice read the same on 0.57.0 and 0.58.0;
  - the new in-flight `verify` id is shown to be harmless;
  - CI's test-only prikk becomes 0.58.0;
  - the record is updated.
- **No feature is added, and the scope does not grow.** The version and the CHANGELOG heading stay.
- **The dates hold:**

  | Target | Step |
  |---|---|
  | 2026-11-20 | M50 – M55 handed back |
  | 2026-11-21 | The review, and the gates on 0.55.0, shimmed off, 0.57.0 and 0.58.0 |
  | 2026-11-22 | The rehearsal on the new candidate, and the cut proposal again (section 7's table, re-measured) |
  | **2026-11-23** | **0.3.0, on your authorization** |
